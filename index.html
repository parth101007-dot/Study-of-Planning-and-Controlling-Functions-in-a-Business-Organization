02 / MANAGEMENT FUNCTION

Planning: prepare before action.

Planning is a primary management function that involves setting objectives and choosing the best course of action to achieve them.

01

Set objectives

Define clear, specific, and measurable goals.

02

Analyze the situation

Review available resources, market conditions, and possible constraints.

03

Identify alternatives

Develop different ways to reach the objective.

04

Evaluate and select

Compare cost, risk, time, and expected results before choosing a plan.

05

Implement and review

Assign responsibilities, communicate the plan, and update it when needed.

KEY TAKEAWAY✦

A good plan turns a broad goal into practical action.

For example, “increase sales” is vague. “Increase monthly sales by 10% in three months through customer follow-ups and a local promotion” is more measurable.

ClarityClear direction
EfficiencyBetter resource use
ReadinessPrepare for risk
CoordinationAligned teams

+ Importance of planning

  • Provides direction and reduces uncertainty
  • Helps coordinate departments and teams
  • Supports efficient use of money, time, and people
  • Creates standards for measuring performance

− Limitations of planning

  • Can take time and involve extra cost
  • Depends on assumptions about the future
  • Rigid plans may reduce flexibility
  • Unexpected changes can make plans outdated
03 / MANAGEMENT FUNCTION

Controlling: measure, learn, improve.

Controlling ensures that actual activities and results are consistent with established plans, objectives, and standards.

+ Importance of controlling

  • Checks whether objectives are being achieved
  • Identifies errors and performance gaps early
  • Improves accountability and resource use
  • Supports quality and continuous improvement

− Limitations of controlling

  • Excessive control can reduce employee initiative
  • Measurement systems may be costly
  • Incorrect standards can produce misleading results
  • Some qualitative outcomes are hard to measure
04 / HOW THEY WORK TOGETHER

A continuous management cycle.

Planning and controlling are closely connected. Planning sets the expected result, and controlling uses evidence to check progress and improve the next plan.

01PLANSet objectives
→
02DOExecute activities
→
03CHECKMeasure results
→
04IMPROVECorrect and learn
↻

Control closes the loop.

Without planning, there are no clear standards to compare against. Without controlling, managers cannot know whether the plan is working. Feedback makes future planning more realistic.

06 / CONCLUSION

Effective management needs both direction and feedback.

This micro project shows that planning and controlling are complementary functions of management. Planning provides objectives and a course of action, while controlling measures results against standards and supports corrective action. When used together, they help a business coordinate work, use resources efficiently, and continuously improve performance.
